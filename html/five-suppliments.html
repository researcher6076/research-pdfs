Five Supplements for Energy and Mitochondrial Health

Daily oral: NMN 1 g; ALCAR 1 g; CoQ10 200 mg; EPA/DHA 660/440 mg; creatine 3 g

NMN, acetyl-L-carnitine (ALCAR), CoQ10, omega-3 fatty acids, and creatine are proposed to support energy, exercise performance, recovery, cognition, and mood, with selected benefits demonstrated for individual ingredients. [3,8,11,12,15,24] NMN is a precursor of NAD+, which participates in cellular redox reactions and NAD+-dependent signaling. [9,37,38] ALCAR participates in acetyl-group metabolism, while CoQ10 transfers electrons within mitochondria. [23,4] Omega-3 fatty acids influence membranes and inflammatory signaling, and creatine buffers ATP availability. [6,12,14] Their experimental combination has plausible metabolic synergies.

Safety depends on the ingredient, dose, population, and exposure duration. [10–12,20,21,24,36]

⭐ Plausibly effective as a combination. Evidence for individual outcomes ranges from established benefits to plausible mechanistic synergies.

Functional Benefits

Energy

CoQ10 reduced fatigue on average across randomized trials involving differing populations and regimens. [11] A benefit for fatigue is likely; improved daily physical and mental energy in otherwise healthy users is plausible.

Endurance and Fatigue Resistance

In 48 amateur runners undertaking exercise training, six weeks of NMN improved selected ventilatory-threshold measures, particularly in the 600- and 1,200-mg/day groups; maximal oxygen uptake did not improve relative to placebo. [8] The broader synthesis likewise supports endpoint-specific rather than uniform functional benefits. [34] Improved stamina during sustained physical activity is plausible.

Recovery and Training Capacity

Omega-3 supplementation can reduce muscle soreness and improve selected recovery measures after eccentric exercise, although effects vary across studies. [15] A trial in 24 men using 600 mg EPA plus 260 mg DHA daily for eight weeks found better strength and range-of-motion recovery and less soreness. [29] Improved recovery and training capacity is plausible.

Cognition and Mental Clarity

Creatine monohydrate improved selected cognitive outcomes during sleep deprivation in small trials using single doses of 0.2–0.35 g/kg. [28,33] Reviews report mixed cognitive findings, and the EFSA assessment did not establish a general cognitive benefit. [2,27,35] Improved mental clarity, less brain fog, and quicker recall at 3 g/day remain plausible.

Inflammation and Resilience

Omega-3 fatty acids influence inflammatory signaling and can provide anti-inflammatory benefits in selected conditions. [6] Support for inflammatory regulation is a plausible.

Mood

ALCAR reduced depressive symptoms in trials of adults with depression, with stronger findings in some older populations and substantial variation between studies. [24,31] Benefit in selected patients is likely; improvement of mood in otherwise healthy users is plausible.

Magnitude and Timing

Benefits can develop over weeks to months. Creatine monohydrate at 3 g/day gradually increased muscle creatine stores over 28 days. [13] The cited omega-3 recovery and mitochondrial studies used supplementation periods of eight and twelve weeks, respectively. [29,14] Timing varies by ingredient and outcome, and performance improvements also depend on training and baseline status. [12]

Biological Mechanisms

Mitochondrial Energy Production

The supplements act on complementary pathways involved in cellular energy metabolism. NMN raised blood-cell NAD+ and improved muscle insulin sensitivity. [9] Mouse studies demonstrated improved mitochondrial oxidative metabolism and NAD+-dependent DNA-repair activity. [37,38]. ALCAR participates in acetyl-group handling and helps maintain the balance between acetyl-CoA and free coenzyme A, making improved cellular energy production through supplementation plausible. [23] CoQ10 transfers electrons from complexes I and II to complex III in the mitochondrial electron transport chain and acts as an antioxidant. [4] EPA plus DHA altered skeletal-muscle mitochondrial membranes and increased ADP sensitivity. [14] Creatine increases muscle creatine stores, and phosphocreatine helps regenerate ATP from ADP during high energy demand. [12,13] T

Oxygen Utilization and Metabolic Efficiency

CoQ10’s role in mitochondrial electron transport makes improved oxygen utilization through supplementation plausible. [4] NMN restores nitric-oxide-dependent neurovascular coupling improves related endothelial signaling. [19]

Neuronal Energy and Cognitive Function

Creatine participates in neuronal ATP buffering. [25,28,33] ALCAR participates in acetyl-group metabolism relevant to neuronal function. [3,23] DHA is a component of neuronal membranes. [20]

Cellular Repair and Stress Resilience

NMN improved mitochondrial oxidative metabolism and NAD+-dependent DNA-repair activity. [37,38] ALCAR’s role in acetyl-group metabolism makes improved recovery from metabolic stress plausible. [23] CoQ10 limits lipid peroxidation, while omega-3 fatty acids alter membrane composition and can reduce soreness after eccentric exercise. [4,14,15]

Inflammation and Redox Signaling

Omega-3 fatty acids influence eicosanoid and resolvin pathways and can provide anti-inflammatory benefits in selected conditions. [6] CoQ10 acts as an antioxidant and helps limit lipid peroxidation. [4] NMN and ALCAR act on pathways involved in cellular metabolism and repair. [19,23]

Synergy

The combination has six plausible synergy patterns based on complementary mechanisms.

NAD+ and Complementary Metabolic Roles

NMN can raise NAD+ levels in blood cells, while ALCAR, CoQ10, and omega-3 fatty acids contribute to acetyl-group metabolism, electron transport, and membrane function. [9,23,4,14]

Energy continuum

ALCAR participates in acetyl-group metabolism, creatine buffers cytosolic ATP, and CoQ10 supports mitochondrial electron transport. [23,12,4]

Membrane and signaling synergy

Omega-3 fatty acids influence membrane composition and the environment of membrane proteins. [14] Synergy with the roles of NMN, ALCAR, and CoQ10 in cellular metabolism is plausible.

Redox stabilization synergy

CoQ10 helps limit lipid peroxidation, while omega-3 fatty acids influence inflammatory signaling. [4,6] Synergy between these roles and the metabolic roles of NMN and ALCAR is plausible.

Neuronal energy synergy

Creatine buffers neuronal ATP, ALCAR participates in acetyl-group metabolism, and DHA contributes to neuronal membrane structure and function. [25,23,20]

Recovery and repair synergy

NMN supports NAD+-dependent pathways, ALCAR participates in acetyl-group metabolism, CoQ10 acts as an antioxidant, and omega-3 fatty acids contribute to membrane composition. [9,23,4,14]

Compatibility

NMN, ALCAR, CoQ10, creatine, and omega-3 fatty acids act on complementary aspects of cellular energy metabolism. [9,23,4,12,14]

Cautions

Higher doses do not necessarily provide greater benefits and can increase adverse effects. [20,40,42]

CoQ10 may interact with warfarin or insulin and may be incompatible with some cancer treatments; use should be discussed with the treating clinician. [21]

Omega-3 use also warrants medication review when anticoagulants are prescribed. [20]

Creatine may modestly increase serum creatinine without reducing measured filtration. [36] Human studies reviewed over months to years support favorable renal tolerability; continued low renal risk at 3 g/day is likely in healthy adults with comparable conditions. [12,36] Kidney disease requires individualized assessment. Maintain normal hydration, and interpret creatinine-based estimates in light of supplementation. [12,36]

Omega-3 fatty acids affect platelet biology, but clinical trials generally have not shown a clinically important bleeding increase at conventional supplemental intakes. [20]

Carnitine-related TMAO production is a mechanistic cardiovascular concern whose clinical significance, especially for ALCAR, remains uncertain. [40]

Side Effects

Several of these supplements can cause digestive upset.

CoQ10 can cause insomnia. [21]

If You Can Only Pick Three

If you can only choose three supplements, prioritize evidence relevant to your goals. Creatine has strong evidence for high-intensity exercise performance. [12] CoQ10 can reduce fatigue, and omega-3 fatty acids can reduce soreness after eccentric exercise. [11,15] NMN has improved selected ventilatory-threshold measures during training, while ALCAR has been studied in specific cognitive and mood disorders. [8,34,3,24]

Optional Additional Supplements

These three supplements are optional additions with different potential applications.

Apigenin inhibits CD38 in cell studies and has increased NAD+ levels in mice. [16] These findings make NAD+ preservation and synergy with NMN plausible in humans.

PQQ stimulates mitochondrial biogenesis through CREB phosphorylation and PGC-1α-related signaling in cell studies. [7] A small human study reported changes in inflammatory and mitochondrial-related metabolic markers. [17] Improved human mitochondrial function through supplementation is plausible. A placebo-controlled trial using PQQ disodium salt at 20 mg/day for 12 weeks reported improved composite and verbal memory. [32] Broader cognitive benefits are possible.

Beta-alanine raises muscle carnosine, which buffers acidity during intense exercise. Supplementation can improve performance in high-intensity efforts lasting roughly one to four minutes. Commonly studied regimens use 4–6 g daily in divided doses for at least two to four weeks. [18]

Choose Supplements with Verified Quality

Choose supplements with clearly stated ingredient amounts and independent testing for identity, potency, and contaminants. Standardized manufacturing can improve consistency, while evidence for the specific formulation and dose helps assess likely benefits. Labels such as “clinically formulated” do not by themselves establish quality, bioavailability, or effectiveness. [22]
