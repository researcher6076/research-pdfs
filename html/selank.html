Selank Available for Research Use Only

(Intranasal: 150–900 mcg/day speculative; 2.7 mg/day adjunct study, 14–21 days)

Selank is a synthetic tuftsin-derived heptapeptide developed in Russia for intranasal use. Its proposed effects involve GABAergic modulation, enkephalin metabolism, neurotrophic signaling, and immune regulation. [5,6,7,8,9,10,11] In small comparative clinical trials Selank reduced symptoms of anxiety with relatively little sedation; improved attention and stress resilience are plausible related benefits. [1,2,3]

💉💉Well tolerated during studied short courses; long-term risks remain incompletely characterized. [1,2,3,21]

⭐⭐Probably effective for anxiety. [1,2,3]

⭐Plausible cognitive support and stress resilience, especially when anxiety or stress impairs performance. [2,3,4,12,13,18,23]

Small clinical studies support relatively favorable short-course tolerability and less sedation than benzodiazepine comparators. [1,2,3] In the 2015 adjunctive study, reduced concentration impairment and fatigue were principally reductions in phenazepam-associated effects, not proof that Selank lacks adverse effects. [3] Rapid peptide degradation makes persistent accumulation less plausible, but does not establish long-term neurological, reproductive, immune, or organ safety. [22]

Dosing Patterns

Intranasal administration can produce acute central effects: a small healthy-volunteer study found changes in functional brain connectivity after administration, an imaging endpoint rather than a demonstration of better cognition. [4] Direct nose-to-brain transport through olfactory or trigeminal pathways is a plausible delivery mechanism. [9] Subjective onset within 15–60 minutes is anecdotal.

The following intranasal patterns are speculative experimental community patterns.

  • 150–300 mcg per administration, one to three times daily; total 150–900 mcg daily

An open prospective study randomly assigned 70 adults to phenazepam alone (30 participants) or phenazepam plus Selank 2.7 mg/day (40 participants) for 14 days; 20 of the latter continued Selank alone through day 21. This was an adjunctive protocol, not a dose-ranging monotherapy trial. [3] A reproduced Russian product instruction specifies two nasal drops in each nostril three times daily for 14 days, with a possible repeat course after 1–3 weeks following medical consultation. Product-specific drop instructions should not be transferred to a different spray device. [22]

Benefits

  • Anxiety reduction: Selank probably reduces anxiety in the studied anxiety/neurasthenia populations, with relatively little sedation in small active-comparator studies. [1,2]
  • Stress resilience: a protective effect is plausible from clinical anxiety findings and animal stress research. [1,2,3,18,23]
  • Cognitive support: improved attention and memory are plausible, particularly when anxiety, drug effects, or stress impair performance. [2,3,12,13]
  • Neurotrophic signaling: Support for adaptive plasticity is plausible, rather than a uniform increase in BDNF. [8,13]
  • Immune modulation: cytokine and immune-gene effects have been observed in human immune studies and animal experiments. Their clinical consequences depend on the tissue and challenge; they do not imply uniformly enhanced immunity. [5,18,20]
  • Antiviral activity: activity against influenza A(H3N2) was observed in cell and mouse experiments, strongest with preventive administration. A corresponding human antiviral benefit is plausible. [14]

Community reports: calm, focus, and sleep benefits are anecdotal and should be distinguished from the measured outcomes above.

Potential Applications

  • Anxiety disorders [1,2,3]
  • Situational anxiety [1,2,23]
  • Stress-related cognitive impairment [2,3,12,13,18]
  • Post-stroke recovery [19,20]

Cautions

  • Pregnancy, breastfeeding, and children: the Russian product instruction contraindicates use during pregnancy or breastfeeding and under age 18. [22]
  • Unstable psychiatric conditions: caution is appropriate in psychosis or bipolar mania. Major psychiatric comorbidities were excluded from the 2015 study, so its tolerability findings should not be generalized to these populations. [3]
  • CNS depressants and psychoactive polypharmacy: interactions may be context dependent. Selank altered olanzapine-associated gene responses in cultured cells and modified benzodiazepine effects in animal and small human studies. These findings support caution, not a predictable interaction magnitude for an individual combination. [3,10,11,23]

Side Effects

  • Sleep effects: sleep disturbance or vivid dreams are anecdotal reports, particularly with late dosing.
  • Activation: transient anxiety or activation is reported anecdotally; distinguish this from the generally favorable short-course tolerability of the studied populations. [1,2,3]

Biological Mechanism

Selank combines the tuftsin sequence Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg with Pro-Gly-Pro and has multimodal biological effects. [9,23] Binding experiments support a proposed allosteric influence on GABAergic signaling. Rat frontal-cortex transcriptional changes partly overlapped those induced by GABA, but the pooled-tissue design limits assessment of between-animal variability. In IMR-32 cells, Selank alone did not significantly alter the measured genes; its combinations with GABA or olanzapine produced different responses. These findings favor context-dependent modulation rather than a simple, uniformly stimulatory GABA mechanism. [9,10,11] Inhibition of plasma enkephalin degradation provides a separate plausible mechanism, with phenotype-dependent behavioral/enzyme effects in mice and reduced morphine-withdrawal signs in rats. [6,7,16,17] Rat learning and ethanol-impairment experiments also implicate monoamine metabolism and context-dependent BDNF regulation. [8,12,13] Human cytokine observations and animal stress/ischemia transcriptomics support immune modulation. [5,18,20] Together, these pathways plausibly influence plasticity, neuroprotection, and anxiety/stress responses; their contributions need not be identical across physiological states.

Combining Selank and Semax

Combining Selank and Semax is a speculative community practice. Their partly distinct but overlapping central effects make complementary effects on anxiety, stress resilience, attention, and learning plausible. The healthy-volunteer connectivity study examined the peptides separately. [4] Additive activation, sleep disturbance, or mood effects are plausible. Specific combined doses, sequencing strategies, alternating-day schedules, and cycling patterns remain speculative.
