Five Supplements for Energy and Mitochondrial Health

These five oral supplements, NMN, acetyl-L-carnitine (ALCAR), CoQ10, omega-3 fatty acids, and creatine, support different aspects of cellular energy metabolism. Human studies support selected benefits of individual ingredients. Synergies in the combination are plausible.

Disclaimer: These notes are not authoritative and should not be interpreted as definitive scientific guidance. They are summaries, compilations and plausible extrapolations drawn from the most relevant research I was able to locate, and may omit nuances, conflicting data, or emerging evidence. The material reflects an attempt to organize and understand complex topics, not to provide clinical recommendations or expert interpretation.

Functional Benefits

Energy

CoQ10 can reduce fatigue. [11] Improved daily physical and mental energy is plausible.

Endurance and Fatigue Resistance

NMN supplementation can improve selected measures of aerobic capacity at ventilatory thresholds during exercise training. [8,34] Improved stamina during sustained physical activity is plausible.

Recovery and Training Capacity

Omega-3 supplementation can reduce muscle soreness and improve selected measures of recovery after eccentric exercise. A small trial found benefits with 600 mg EPA plus 260 mg DHA daily for eight weeks. [15,29] Improved recovery and training capacity is plausible.

Cognition and Mental Clarity

Creatine monohydrate improved selected cognitive outcomes during sleep deprivation in trials using single doses of 0.2–0.35 g/kg. Cognitive benefits at 3 g/day are plausible. [28,33] Improved mental clarity, less brain fog, and quicker recall are plausible.

Inflammation and Resilience

Omega-3 fatty acids influence inflammatory signaling and can provide anti-inflammatory benefits in selected conditions. [6] Support for inflammatory regulation is plausible benefit.

Mood

ALCAR can reduce depressive symptoms in adults with depression, with stronger findings in some older populations. [24,31]

Magnitude and Timing

Benefits can develop over weeks to months. Creatine monohydrate at 3 g/day gradually increased muscle creatine stores over 28 days. The cited omega-3 recovery and mitochondrial studies used supplementation periods of eight and twelve weeks, respectively. [13,14,29] Timing varies by ingredient and outcome, and performance improvements also depend on training and baseline status.

Biological Mechanisms

Mitochondrial Energy Production

The supplements act on complementary pathways involved in cellular energy metabolism. NMN supplementation can raise NAD+ levels in blood cells. [9] NMN likely supports mitochondrial function and DNA repair, based on improved mitochondrial oxidative metabolism and NAD+-dependent DNA-repair activity in mouse studies. [37,38] ALCAR participates in acetyl-group handling and helps maintain the balance between acetyl-CoA and free coenzyme A. [23] These roles make improved cellular energy production through supplementation plausible. CoQ10 transfers electrons from complexes I and II to complex III in the mitochondrial electron transport chain and acts as an antioxidant. [4] These roles make improved mitochondrial efficiency through supplementation plausible. In a human skeletal-muscle study, 2,000 mg EPA plus 1,000 mg DHA daily for 12 weeks altered mitochondrial membrane composition and improved responsiveness to ADP, which helps regulate ATP production. Similar effects at 660 mg EPA plus 440 mg DHA daily are plausible. [14] Creatine supplementation increases muscle creatine and phosphocreatine stores. Phosphocreatine helps regenerate ATP from ADP during periods of high energy demand. [12,13] Together, these complementary mechanisms make improved cellular energy availability plausible.

Oxygen Utilization and Metabolic Efficiency

Improved oxygen utilization and metabolic efficiency are plausible benefits of the combination. CoQ10’s role in mitochondrial electron transport makes improved oxygen utilization through supplementation plausible. [4] Animal research suggests NMN is likely to support endothelial nitric oxide signaling and blood flow, potentially improving oxygen delivery. [19] Together, these mechanisms make improved stamina during sustained physical activity plausible.

Neuronal Energy and Cognitive Function

Several components of the combination have roles in neuronal energy metabolism. Creatine helps buffer neuronal ATP. Creatine monohydrate improved selected cognitive outcomes during sleep deprivation in trials using single doses of 0.2–0.35 g/kg. Cognitive benefits at 3 g/day are plausible. [28,33] ALCAR participates in acetyl-group metabolism relevant to neuronal function. [3,23] These roles make improved neuronal energy metabolism and resilience through supplementation plausible. DHA is a component of neuronal membranes and contributes to their structure and function. [20] Improved synaptic function through omega-3 supplementation is plausible. Together, these mechanisms make reduced brain fog, improved recall, and sustained mental clarity plausible benefits of the combination.

Cellular Repair and Stress Resilience

Improved cellular repair and resilience to metabolic stress are plausible benefits of the combination. NMN likely supports mitochondrial function and DNA repair, based on improved mitochondrial oxidative metabolism and NAD+-dependent DNA-repair activity in mouse studies. [37,38] ALCAR’s role in acetyl-group metabolism makes improved recovery from metabolic stress plausible. [23] CoQ10 protects mitochondrial membranes from oxidative damage and lipid peroxidation, while omega-3 fatty acids contribute to membrane composition and can reduce muscle soreness after eccentric exercise. [4,14,15] Together, these mechanisms make improved cellular recovery and resilience to physical and metabolic stress plausible.

Inflammation and Redox Signaling

The ingredients also act on pathways involved in inflammation and redox regulation. Omega-3 fatty acids influence eicosanoid and resolvin pathways and can provide anti-inflammatory benefits in selected conditions. [6] CoQ10 acts as an antioxidant and helps limit lipid peroxidation. [4] NMN and ALCAR act on pathways involved in cellular metabolism and repair, making an indirect reduction in inflammatory burden plausible. [19,23] Together, these actions make support for inflammatory regulation a plausible benefit of the combination.

Synergy

The combination has six plausible synergy patterns based on complementary mechanisms.

NAD+ and Complementary Metabolic Roles

NMN can raise NAD+ levels in blood cells, while ALCAR, CoQ10, and omega-3 fatty acids contribute to acetyl-group metabolism, electron transport, and membrane function. [9,23,4,14] Synergy between these complementary roles is plausible.

Energy continuum

ALCAR participates in acetyl-group metabolism, creatine buffers cytosolic ATP, and CoQ10 supports mitochondrial electron transport. [23,12,4] Synergy between these roles in maintaining cellular energy availability is plausible.

Membrane and signaling synergy

Omega-3 fatty acids influence membrane composition and the environment of membrane proteins. [14] Synergy with the roles of NMN, ALCAR, and CoQ10 in cellular metabolism is plausible.

Redox stabilization synergy

CoQ10 helps limit lipid peroxidation, while omega-3 fatty acids influence inflammatory signaling. [4,6] Synergy between these roles and the metabolic roles of NMN and ALCAR is plausible.

Neuronal energy synergy

Creatine buffers neuronal ATP, ALCAR participates in acetyl-group metabolism, and DHA contributes to neuronal membrane structure and function. [25,23,20] Synergy between these roles in supporting cognition and resilience under metabolic stress is plausible.

Recovery and repair synergy

NMN supports NAD+-dependent pathways, ALCAR participates in acetyl-group metabolism, CoQ10 acts as an antioxidant, and omega-3 fatty acids contribute to membrane composition. [9,23,4,14] Synergy between these roles in supporting recovery and cellular resilience is plausible.

Compatibility

NMN, ALCAR, CoQ10, creatine, and omega-3 fatty acids act on complementary aspects of cellular energy metabolism. Their mechanisms make compatibility within the combination plausible.

Cautions

Discuss CoQ10 use with the prescribing clinician if taking warfarin or insulin, because interactions may occur. [21]

Maintain normal hydration when using creatine. If you have kidney disease or risk factors for it, discuss use and appropriate kidney-function monitoring with a clinician. [12]

Higher doses do not necessarily provide greater benefits and may increase the risk of adverse effects.

If You Can Only Pick Three

If you can only choose three supplements, prioritize those with evidence relevant to your goals. Creatine has strong evidence for high-intensity exercise performance, CoQ10 can reduce fatigue, and omega-3 fatty acids can reduce muscle soreness after eccentric exercise. [12,11,15] NMN has shown benefits in selected measures of aerobic capacity at ventilatory thresholds during exercise training, while ALCAR has been studied in specific cognitive and clinical conditions. [8,34,3]

Optional Additional Supplements

These three supplements are optional additions with different evidence scopes and potential uses.

Apigenin inhibits CD38 in cell studies and has increased NAD+ levels in mice. [16] These findings make NAD+ preservation and synergy with NMN plausible in humans.

PQQ stimulates mitochondrial biogenesis through PGC-1α-related signaling in cell studies. [7] Improved mitochondrial function through supplementation is plausible in humans. In a placebo-controlled human trial, PQQ at 20 mg/day for 12 weeks improved composite and verbal memory. [32]

Beta-alanine raises muscle carnosine, which buffers acidity during intense exercise. Supplementation can improve performance in high-intensity efforts lasting roughly one to four minutes. Commonly studied regimens use 4–6 g daily in divided doses for at least two to four weeks; divided doses can reduce tingling. [18]

Choose Supplements with Verified Quality

Choose supplements with clearly stated ingredient amounts and independent testing for identity, potency, and contaminants. Standardized manufacturing can improve consistency, while evidence for the specific formulation and dose helps assess likely benefits. Labels such as “clinically formulated” do not by themselves establish quality, bioavailability, or effectiveness. [22]
