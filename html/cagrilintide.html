Cagrilintide

0.3–4.5 mg SC once weekly, dose-escalated

Cagrilintide is an experimental, long-acting amylin analogue that reduces body weight in human trials; combining it with semaglutide produces greater weight loss than either component alone. [1,5] It activates amylin receptors and the calcitonin receptor in laboratory settings. [9,10] Animal studies identify brainstem feeding circuits that help explain its likely effects on human appetite and satiety. [4,7] Its lipidated design prolongs exposure and supports once-weekly administration. [2,9]

💉💉💉Human trials characterize tolerability through 68 weeks; gastrointestinal effects are common, and safety depends on the regimen and endpoint. [1,5,6]

⭐⭐⭐ Effective for weight reduction in studied populations (randomized human trials). [1,5,6]

Once-weekly cagrilintide has demonstrated weight loss as monotherapy and in the semaglutide combination, CagriSema. These are distinct interventions; combination outcomes should not be attributed to cagrilintide alone. [1,3,5,6]

In REDEFINE 1, adults with overweight or obesity without diabetes lost an estimated 20.4% of body weight with CagriSema, 11.5% with cagrilintide, 14.9% with semaglutide, and 3.0% with placebo at 68 weeks under the treatment-policy analysis, which includes treatment discontinuation. Both drugs targeted 2.4 mg weekly. [5]

In the sponsor’s February 2026 REDEFINE 4 announcement, CagriSema 2.4/2.4 mg did not meet the primary endpoint versus tirzepatide 15 mg. Estimated weight loss at 84 weeks was 23.0% versus 25.5% under the efficacy estimand, which assumes treatment adherence. These are sponsor-reported results from an open-label comparison. [8]

Dose

Cagrilintide is administered as a once-weekly subcutaneous (SC) injection. In the phase 1b combination study, its terminal half-life was approximately 7 days with median peak concentrations reached after 24–72 hours. [2]

Doses Studied as Monotherapy

The 26-week phase 2 monotherapy trial in adults with overweight or obesity without diabetes evaluated these weekly target doses: [1]

  • 0.3 mg once weekly
  • 0.6 mg once weekly
  • 1.2 mg once weekly
  • 2.4 mg once weekly
  • 4.5 mg once weekly

Mean weight loss increased across the tested doses, reaching 10.8% with 4.5 mg versus 3.0% with placebo under the trial-product analysis, which assumes continued treatment. The trial used dose escalation; the listed targets were not all starting doses. [1]

Dose Used with Semaglutide

The target regimen in the published REDEFINE 1 and 2 phase 3 CagriSema trials was: [5,6]

  • Cagrilintide 2.4 mg + semaglutide 2.4 mg SC once weekly

Treatment was escalated toward this target, with flexibility for tolerability. [5,6] The published REIMAGINE 2 trial also studied CagriSema 1.0/1.0 mg weekly in type 2 diabetes. [14] In September 2026, the sponsor reported additional phase 3 results with this lower-dose combination, including a comparison with tirzepatide 5 mg; that comparison differs from the higher-dose REDEFINE 4 trial. [8,12]

The earlier phase 1b study tested cagrilintide up to 4.5 mg weekly with semaglutide 2.4 mg; 2.4 mg is therefore the principal REDEFINE target, not the highest combination dose ever studied. [2]

Dose Escalation

Gradual escalation helps manage gastrointestinal intolerance, including nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and constipation. Tolerability varies between individuals and regimens. [1,2,6]

The clinical trial approach was: low starting exposure → gradual escalation → individually tolerated maintenance exposure.

In REDEFINE 1, each component began at 0.25 mg weekly and was increased at four-week intervals toward 2.4 mg by week 16; delayed escalation and dose reductions were permitted. Escalation depended on tolerability, rather than an obligation to reach the largest studied dose. [5,6]

Frequency

The approximately one-week half-life means appreciable drug remains when the next weekly dose is given. [2] The trial results support weekly administration. [1,5,6] Splitting the weekly dose into multiple administrations is a speculative community practice.

Benefits

  • Dose-dependent weight loss — demonstrated in the phase 2 and phase 3 monotherapy trials. [1,5]
  • Likely appetite suppression and increased satiety — supported by Cagrilintide’s effects on feeding and satiety circuits in animals, alongside human weight-loss efficacy. [1,4,7]
  • Plausible earlier meal termination — amylin reduced meal size and duration in rats, supporting this proposed contributor to cagrilintide’s appetite effects. [7,15]
  • Greater weight loss with semaglutide — established for CagriSema versus either component in randomized trials. [3,5] In REDEFINE 2, adults with type 2 diabetes lost 13.7% versus 3.4% with placebo at 68 weeks under the treatment-policy analysis. [6]
  • Plausibly complementary appetite signaling — different amylin and GLP-1 targets provide a biological explanation for the combination benefit. [7,10]
  • Improved glycemic control with CagriSema in type 2 diabetes — demonstrated in human trials. [3,6] In the small 32-week phase 2 trial, HbA1c fell by 2.2 percentage points with CagriSema, 0.9 with cagrilintide, and 1.8 with semaglutide. [3]

Investigated Uses

  • Obesity and/or weight-related comorbidities — supported by human weight-management trials, alongside lifestyle intervention. [1,5,6]
  • Combination with semaglutide — directly studied for additional weight reduction and glycemic control in the relevant trial populations. [3,5,6,14]
  • Metabolic and appetite endpoints — animal and receptor studies investigate amylin signaling, feeding behavior, and its interaction with GLP-1 pathways. [4,7,9,10]

Cautions

  • Pregnancy and breastfeeding — safety of cagrilintide in these populations is not established. [1,5,6] Semaglutide used for weight reduction is discontinued when pregnancy is recognized and at least two months before a planned pregnancy; lactation requires an individualized assessment. [13]
  • Active eating disorders or inadequate nutrition — appetite reduction makes nutritional and eating-disorder assessment a clinical precaution. [1,4]
  • Severe gastroparesis — a mechanistic caution for Cagrilintide because amylin pathways can influence gastric emptying; semaglutide’s prescribing information explicitly advises against its use in severe gastroparesis. [7,13]
  • Unstable cardiovascular disease — weight-management trials do not establish safety in unstable disease or cardiovascular-event prevention. [5,6] CagriSema lowered blood pressure in a REDEFINE 1 analysis; that intermediate outcome should be distinguished from prevention of cardiovascular events. [11]

Side effects

  • Gastrointestinal effects — nausea, vomiting, constipation, and diarrhea occur in human trials and are commonly mild or moderate. [1,5,6]
  • Reduced intake and dehydration — appetite suppression or gastrointestinal symptoms can compromise food or fluid intake. [1,6,13]
  • Heart rate depends on the regimen — in REDEFINE 1, mean pulse changed by −3.31 beats/min with cagrilintide, +0.94 with CagriSema, +1.17 with semaglutide, and −0.58 with placebo at 68 weeks. [5]
  • Hypoglycemia with other glucose-lowering medicines — semaglutide increases risk when combined with insulin or insulin secretagogues such as sulfonylureas. This matters for CagriSema and requires medication review and glucose monitoring; it is not proof of the same risk magnitude for cagrilintide alone. [13]
  • Gallbladder and pancreatic events — gallbladder events occurred in 2.3% with cagrilintide, 4.1% with CagriSema, and 1.0% with placebo in REDEFINE 1. Semaglutide also carries pancreatitis and gallbladder warnings when used in the combination. [5,13]

Biological mechanism

Cagrilintide activates amylin receptors AMY1, AMY2, and AMY3, which consist of the calcitonin receptor paired with receptor activity-modifying proteins RAMP1, RAMP2, and RAMP3, respectively. It also activates the calcitonin receptor without a RAMP. These findings come from receptor pharmacology experiments. [9,10]

Animal studies support central control of feeding through the dorsal vagal complex and connected circuits. The 2026 cross-species atlas studied mice, rats, and macaques and identified calcitonin-receptor/prolactin-releasing-peptide neurons also present in human brainstem tissue. Rat experiments implicated nucleus tractus solitarius Prlh signaling in sustained cagrilintide responses. These conserved circuits support a likely contribution to human satiety. [7]

Mouse receptor-deletion experiments identify AMY1 and AMY3 signaling as important contributors to cagrilintide-induced weight loss; corresponding contributions in humans are mechanistic extrapolations. [4]

Amylin and GLP-1 agonism engage partly distinct feeding pathways. This is a plausible mechanistic explanation for the greater weight loss established with Cagrilintide plus semaglutide. [3,5,7,10]

Lipidation prolongs exposure, with albumin binding proposed as a contributing mechanism. [9] The measured human terminal half-life of 159–195 hours supports once-weekly dosing but is not a direct measure of receptor occupancy. [2]
