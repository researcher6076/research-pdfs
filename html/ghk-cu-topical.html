GHK Cu Available for Research Use Only

(1 mg / day for cosmetic effects, 2 mg / day for tissue remodeling)

⭐⭐ ⭐ Effective for improving several measures of photoaged facial skin.

⭐⭐ ⭐ Effective for increasing hair growth and thickness.

⭐⭐ Likely to support local wound repair and extracellular-matrix remodeling based on injected animal evidence.

⭐⭐ Safety is likely based on animal evidence and common use in cosmetics.

Formulation

An ideal formulation would be 2% GHK-Cu raw powder in an aqueous cosmetic base containing 2% total mixed high- and low-molecular-weight hyaluronic acid.

Use Patterns

  • Apply twice daily. Once-daily applications are a practical lower-frequency pattern.
  • Apply a thin, even layer to clean skin. Avoid direct contact with the eyes and mucosal surfaces.

Benefits

  • Photoaged skin appearance: Topical GHK-Cu improves the overall appearance of photoaged facial and periocular skin, including clarity and visible texture, in human studies using GHK-Cu creams.
  • Fine lines and wrinkles: Topical GHK-Cu reduces facial and periocular lines and wrinkles over approximately 12 weeks in the reported human studies.
  • Skin firmness and density: Topical GHK-Cu improves measured skin density, thickness, firmness, and laxity in the reported facial studies.
  • Uneven pigmentation appearance: Topical GHK-Cu improves mottled pigmentation and visible photodamage in reported human facial studies.
  • Hair growth: Increases hair count and diameterl.
  • Local wound repair: A 2% GHK-Cu gel supported closure of diabetic lower-extremity ulcers when used with debridement and structured wound care. This evidence applies to the studied medical formulation and setting.
  • Collagen synthesis support: GHK-Cu likely promotes local collagen synthesis and extracellular-matrix repair based on injected and topical animal evidence, supported by fibroblast and ex vivo skin findings.
  • Matrix remodeling support: GHK-Cu likely supports regulated remodeling of collagen, glycosaminoglycans, and other matrix components during local repair.
  • Scar appearance support: Improvement in scar texture and appearance is plausible through effects on collagen production, matrix remodeling, inflammation, and wound repair.
  • Basement membrane support: GHK-Cu plausibly supports the dermal-epidermal junction by increasing collagen IV, based on fibroblast and ex vivo skin research.
  • Inflammatory signal regulation: Local modulation of excessive inflammatory signaling is plausible through effects on NF-κB-related and cytokine pathways described in preclinical research.
  • Antioxidant defense support: Local support of antioxidant defenses is plausible through copper-dependent enzymes and GHK-Cu-associated regulation of oxidative-stress pathways.

Cautions

  • Copper allergy, contact sensitivity, or sensitivity to another ingredient in the finished base warrants caution and patch testing.
  • Active dermatitis, a disrupted skin barrier, or recent cosmetic procedures can increase irritation and absorption. Introduce the serum only after the skin has recovered or with clinician guidance.
  • Use on chronic ulcers, burns, infected wounds, or other significant injuries belongs in supervised medical wound care rather than a cosmetic protocol.
  • Application directly over a known or suspected skin cancer warrants clinical guidance because GHK-Cu participates in repair, angiogenic, and matrix-remodeling pathways.
  • Wilson disease or another disorder of copper handling warrants clinician guidance before prolonged or large-area use.

Biological Mechanism

GHK binds copper with high affinity and forms GHK-Cu, a redox-active but biologically regulated complex. In skin models, GHK-Cu influences fibroblast and keratinocyte activity, collagen and glycosaminoglycan production, matrix metalloproteinases and their inhibitors, inflammatory cytokine signaling, antioxidant defenses, angiogenesis, and recruitment of repair processes. These overlapping effects support regulated tissue remodeling rather than a single linear pathway.

Formulation Chemistry with Hyaluronic Acid

An appropriate serum combines 2% GHK-Cu with 2% total mixed-molecular-weight hyaluronic acid in an aqueous cosmetic base. Hyaluronic acid is a water-binding polymer that increases surface hydration and can improve slip and film formation. Higher-molecular-weight fractions primarily support surface hydration and film properties, while lower-molecular-weight fractions interact more readily with superficial skin compartments.

In fibroblast and ex vivo skin experiments, GHK-Cu combined with hyaluronic acid increased collagen I, IV, and VII expression, with the strongest collagen IV response reported for a 1:9 GHK-Cu to low-molecular-weight hyaluronic-acid ratio. This provides a plausible biological rationale for combining the ingredients.
