CJC‑1295 NODAC + Ipamorelin Available for Research Use Only

150–200 mcg each SC once or twice daily; 8–16 weeks on, 4–6 weeks off

CJC-1295 (no DAC) + ipamorelin is an experimental GH-secretagogue combination that likely enhances GH release, with plausible applications in body composition, sleep, and recovery. [2–4,6,8,14,19,20] CJC-1295 (no DAC), commonly called modified GRF(1–29), is a GHRH analog distinct from the long-acting, albumin-binding DAC compound. [4,22,23] Ipamorelin is a ghrelin-receptor (GHSR-1a) agonist that stimulated GH release in human volunteers. [2,6] Complementary receptor signaling provides a plausible basis for synergy and downstream IGF-1 elevation. [3,8] Animal selectivity findings support plausible sparing of cortisol and prolactin responses in humans. [2]

Safety depends on the endpoint, preparation, and exposure duration; the combination requires separate assessment from its individual components. [7,15,23,24]

💉Plausibly safe

⭐⭐Likely enhancement of GH release, with plausible benefits for body composition and recovery. [2–4,6,8,14,15]

C Plausible tumor-growth concern through GH/IGF-1 signaling. Avoid use with active cancer; prior cancer or elevated cancer risk warrants individualized assessment. [17,22]

🔬Testing encouraged — monitor IGF-1 against the age-adjusted reference range; levels above that range warrant reassessment of use and dose. [16,22]

Safety assessment draws on separately administered ipamorelin and the DAC form of CJC-1295. In a postoperative trial with 114 patients in the safety analysis, IV ipamorelin for up to 7 days was described by the investigators as well tolerated; treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 87.5% versus 94.8% with placebo. [7] The surgical population, IV route, and short exposure limit extrapolation to repeated SC combination use. [7,24]

CJC-1295 (no DAC) + ipamorelin likely enhances GH release, with downstream elevation of IGF-1. This inference draws on human ipamorelin findings, GHRH-analog pharmacology, and complementary receptor signaling. [2–4,6,8] The sustained human GH/IGF-1 responses reported for CJC-1295 were obtained with the DAC formulation but plausibly apply to repeated NODAC administration. [1,5]

Body-composition benefits remain plausible from GH/IGF-1 biology. [15] Ipamorelin increased adiposity in mice, including through GH-independent effects, which complicates the fat-loss hypothesis for this combination. [11]

Dosage & Patterns

Human IV ipamorelin research demonstrated dose-dependent GH responses. [6]

The speculative experimental pattern discussed in peptide communities and wellness spas is SC administration once or twice daily, while fasted, before bedtime or immediately after waking. [21,22]

  • 150–200 mcg of each peptide per administration

Reported empirical schedules include 8–12 or 12–16 weeks of use, generally followed by 4–6 weeks off. [22]

  • 8–12 weeks on with 4 weeks off, or 12–16 weeks on with 4–6 weeks off.

Preservation of response through planned breaks remains speculative. [22]

Benefits

  • Plausible improvement in sleep quality through GHRH-related sleep mechanisms. GHRH improved sleep in a study of men but impaired sleep in a study of young women. [19,20] Easier sleep onset and vivid dreaming remain speculative outcomes for this combination.
  • Plausible support for recovery from training through GH/IGF-1-related tissue-repair signaling. [14] Less next-day soreness and a faster return to baseline performance after heavy sessions are speculative functional outcomes of that mechanism.
  • Plausible support for connective-tissue repair, informed by increased tendon collagen synthesis during a 14-day human GH study. [14] A qualitative study of female CJC-1295 users recorded anecdotal joint-comfort improvements in a setting of varied formulations and frequent use of other substances. [21] Tendon resilience and faster soft-tissue healing remain plausible translational outcomes.
  • Improved workout quality and muscle fullness are plausible experiential outcomes of GH-axis stimulation and changes in tissue water or body composition. [15,22] The cited human GH study increased tendon collagen synthesis without increasing myofibrillar protein synthesis. [14]
  • Plausible support for body-composition changes through GH/IGF-1-related signaling. [15] The female-user netnography recorded anecdotal perceptions of looking slimmer, amid multiple co-exposures. [21] Increased adiposity in ipamorelin-treated mice is a counterpoint to an uncomplicated fat-loss expectation. [11]
  • Calmer evenings, greater stress tolerance, and emotional resilience remain speculative extensions to this combination. A qualitative study recorded anecdotal improvements in mood and outlook among female CJC-1295 users. [21]
  • Plausible support for skin quality through GH/IGF-1-related collagen and tissue-remodeling signaling. [14] Female CJC-1295 users described skin changes anecdotally. [21] Improved hydration, elasticity, and tone are speculative specific outcomes.
  • Greater training readiness and reduced cumulative fatigue are speculative functional extensions of recovery-related GH/IGF-1 signaling. [14]

Applications

  • Recovery from training is a proposed use informed by tissue-repair mechanisms. [14,21]
  • Body recomposition is a reported community goal. [21,22]
  • Reported use aimed at age-related declines in GH secretion (age-related decline is distinct from clinically diagnosed GH deficiency). [16,21,22]

Cautions

  • Active malignancy: avoid use because of plausible tumor-supportive GH/IGF-1 signaling. [17,22]
  • Uncontrolled diabetes: GH-related worsening of glycemic control is a material concern. [15,16,24]

Side Effects

  • Potential water retention and joint discomfort. [15,22]
  • Appetite may increase: ipamorelin increased food intake in animal studies, and CJC-1295 users described increased hunger in the qualitative study. [11,13,21] GH stimulation can impair insulin sensitivity and plausibly increase dysglycemia risk. [15,24]

Long Term Safety Signals

Endocrine and metabolic safety: GH-related glucose intolerance is a plausible risk. [15,16] In the postoperative IV ipamorelin trial, FDA reported hyperglycemia at discharge in 14.3% versus 8.6% with placebo and hypokalemia in 12.5% versus 3.4%. These differences arose in a small surgical study and do not establish causation or predict rates with SC combination use. [7,24]

Fluid retention and musculoskeletal safety: Edema and joint discomfort are recognized effects of GH exposure and plausible effects of this combination. [15] Female CJC-1295 users also reported bloating and joint pain during prolonged use; those uncontrolled reports involved variable products and other exposures. [21]

Cardiovascular and neurological safety: FDA noted two deaths in the postoperative ipamorelin group versus none with placebo, with uncertain causal attribution. Insomnia occurred in 10.7% versus 5.2%. [24] DAC-form CJC-1295 studies reported flushing, headache, and transient cardiovascular effects; that formulation has a different exposure profile. [23] These findings support monitoring rather than a confident long-term low-risk inference for the combination.

I

Hepatic and renal safety: Short DAC trials showed no consistent liver-laboratory changes. [23] This observation is specific to the studied preparation and duration. Long-term hepatic and renal tolerability of the SC combination cannot yet be assigned a confident low-risk classification from those findings.

Reproductive and proliferative safety: Reproductive and developmental risks remain insufficiently characterized; ghrelin-receptor biology raises a plausible concern about pregnancy-related outcomes. [24] Tumor-growth mechanisms and clinical GH-replacement experience are assessed separately below. Rare or delayed outcomes require a different inference from common short-term adverse effects. [17]

Drivers of Diminished Response and Mitigation Strategies

Receptor desensitization plausibly contributes to diminished response during repeated stimulation. [3] After 15 days of ipamorelin in female rats, the response to ipamorelin was modestly reduced, while the GHRH response was preserved. [9] A separate 21-day rat study found dynamic changes in somatotroph GH content with retained responses in cell culture. [12] These findings suggest context-dependent adaptation rather than uniform loss of responsiveness. Immunogenicity is a potential concern. [23,24] IGF-1-mediated negative feedback is established physiology and plausibly limits the GH response. [3,16]

Intermittent dosing and planned breaks are empirical practices whose ability to preserve response remains speculative. Periodic IGF-1 monitoring can help assess biochemical response and guide reassessment. [16,22]

CJC-1295 NODAC + Ipamorelin and Cancer Risk

GH/IGF-1 signaling provides a plausible tumor-growth concern, particularly with active or residual malignancy. The concern extends to interventions that chronically raise GH and IGF-1: these pathways support proliferation and resistance to apoptosis in susceptible tumor systems. [17,22]

Acromegaly is associated with increased incidence of certain cancers, particularly colorectal and thyroid cancers. These observational findings support concern about prolonged GH/IGF-1 excess but do not distinguish tumor initiation from promotion or quantify risk from this combination. [18] Endogenous GH stimulation plausibly retains physiological feedback regulation. [3] Its comparative cancer risk depends on the resulting exposure and clinical context; preserved feedback alone is not a measure of oncological safety. [17,22]

Mechanistically, a microscopic tumor or residual disease could gain a growth advantage from increased GH/IGF-1 signaling. Experimental tumor biology supports this concern in susceptible breast, prostate, colorectal, and sarcoma systems; susceptibility differs across tumor types and molecular contexts. [17]

Avoid use with active cancer. Prior cancer, recent treatment, or strong familial or genetic risk warrants individualized specialist assessment rather than a single rule for all patients. [17,22]

The magnitude and duration of GH/IGF-1 elevation plausibly influence tumor-growth signaling. Individual absolute cancer risk from this combination remains unquantified. [17,22]

Biological Mechanism

CJC-1295 (no DAC) acts through the GHRH receptor, while ipamorelin acts through the ghrelin receptor (GHSR-1a). [2–4] The combination likely enhances GH release, with plausible synergy through complementary receptor signaling and plausible downstream elevation of IGF-1. [3,6,8] Limited cortisol and prolactin responses in animal experiments support plausible endocrine selectivity in humans. [2]

Conclusions

CJC-1295 (no DAC) + ipamorelin likely enhances GH release, with plausible benefits for sleep, recovery, connective-tissue support, and body composition. [2–4,6,8,14,15,19,20] Animal adiposity findings and unchanged myofibrillar protein synthesis in the cited human GH study temper expectations for fat loss and muscle-fiber anabolism. [11,14] Fasting, bedtime administration, and intermittent schedules are empirical practices. [21,22] Safety should be assessed separately for glycemic control, fluid retention, immune reactions, and unresolved organ-specific or delayed outcomes. [15–17,23,24] Avoid use with active malignancy; prior cancer or elevated cancer risk warrants individualized specialist assessment. [17] Monitor IGF-1 against the age-adjusted reference range, with reassessment if levels exceed that range. [16,22]
