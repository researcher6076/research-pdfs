KPV Available for Research Use Only

(0.25 mg – 0.5 mg daily, increased to 1 mg to 2 mg daily as needed)

KPV is the C-terminal tripeptide (Lys-Pro-Val) of α-melanocyte-stimulating hormone. It has evidence for reduceing intestinal inflammation and neutrophil recruitment and plausible inhibition of NF-κB and MAPK signaling and support for intestinal epithelial-barrier integrity. [3–5,7,9] Practitioner and community reports describe improvements in gut symptoms and inflammatory discomfort. Practitioner and community reports describe use for gut, skin, and systemic inflammatory symptoms. Broader skin and systemic applications remain plausible. [7,11,12,26]

💉💉Probably safe

⭐⭐Probably effective for selected outcomes supported by animal studies.

KPV’s safety assessment rests on preclinical observations. Tolerability findings from animal studies and engineered KPV formulations provide limited reassurance. Long-term human safety remains uncertain. [4–6,9,27]

KPV likely reduces intestinal inflammation, supported by multiple animal colitis studies, with plausible support for mucosal-barrier integrity. Human cell studies support mechanisms involving PepT1-mediated uptake and inhibition of inflammatory signaling. [3–6,9,14,15]

KPV likely reduces neutrophil recruitment, with plausible support for broader anti-inflammatory effects through inhibition of NF-κB and MAPK signaling and reduced inflammatory mediator production. Community reports describe wider symptom improvements. [3,4,7,11,26]

Dosage & Patterns

There is no evidence-based human dose for KPV. Relying on practitioner and community reports

  • 0.25–0.5 mg daily is a reported use pattern.
  • 1–2 mg/day for short courses, such as 3–14 days, is also reported.
  • Larger doses up to 5 mg/day are occasionally reported, but very much outside mainstream discussions.

Community reports describe continuous KPV use. Its largely melanocortin-receptor-independent actions provide a plausible rationale for a low risk of classical receptor desensitization, but do not establish freedom from tolerance or endocrine effects. Adaptation within inflammatory pathways remains plausible, and long-term human safety remains uncertain. [3–5,7,27]

Benefits

Core anti-inflammatory actions
  • Likely reduction in intestinal inflammatory cytokine activity, including TNF-α and IL-6, with plausible inhibition of NF-κB and MAPK signaling. [4,5,7]
  • Plausible suppression of inflammatory signaling through largely melanocortin-receptor-independent mechanisms. [3,4,7]
Gut and mucosal support
  • Plausible support for intestinal epithelial-barrier integrity through reduced inflammatory signaling. [4,6,9,15]
  • Likely reduction in intestinal inflammation. Community reports describe improvements in IBS-like symptoms, food-triggered irritation, and gluten sensitivity. [4,5,26]
  • Plausible support for intestinal mucosal healing, informed by animal studies of targeted KPV delivery formulations that reduced inflammation and promoted mucosal repair. [6,9,14,15]
  • Likely reduction in inflammation-associated intestinal tumor development, based on a mouse model of colitis-associated cancer in which protection depended on PepT1-mediated uptake. Broader cancer-protective effects remain plausible. [8]
  • Community reports describe less post-meal discomfort after foods that previously triggered symptoms.
Skin and tissue calming
  • Likely reduction in localized inflammatory skin swelling, based on animal findings, with plausible protection against inflammatory skin-cell injury. Practitioner and community reports describe use for eczema, dermatitis, and skin irritation. [11,12,26]
  • Likely support for corneal epithelial wound healing with topical KPV, based on animal findings. Broader support for wound-healing environments through reduced inflammatory signaling remains plausible. [7,24]
Joint and systemic effects
  • Plausible support for reduced joint inflammation through anti-inflammatory signaling. Community reports describe relief of overuse soreness and inflammatory joint discomfort. [3,7,26]
  • Plausible support for broader systemic anti-inflammatory effects through reduced inflammatory signaling. [3,7]
  • Likely attenuation of diet-associated weight gain and adipose-tissue expansion, based on a mouse study using oral KPV during a high-fat diet. [19]
  • Plausible reduction in hepatic lipid accumulation, based on a cultured liver-cell study showing reduced lipid buildup under fatty-acid exposure. [18]
Immune-regulatory effects
  • Plausible reduction in excessive inflammatory signaling through inhibition of NF-κB and MAPK pathways. [4,7]
  • Plausible antimicrobial activity against Staphylococcus aureus and Candida albicans, based on laboratory experiments demonstrating inhibited microbial growth or viability. [23]
  • Community reports describe stacking KPV with thymic peptides. Complementary effects on inflammatory and immune signaling are plausible.

Reported Practitioner and Community Uses

  • Chronic or recurrent inflammation: Post-meal inflammatory flares, histamine-related symptoms, low-grade systemic inflammation
  • Gut-related issues: IBS-like symptoms, food-triggered gut irritation, gluten sensitivity, post-antibiotic gut disruption, “leaky gut”
  • Skin inflammation: Eczema, dermatitis, psoriasis, inflammatory rashes
  • Joint or soft-tissue inflammation: Overuse soreness, mild inflammatory joint discomfort, localized swelling after activity
  • Needing immune-regulatory support: immune systems that “overreact” to triggers, inflammatory flares

Precautions

  • Caution during active, uncontrolled infections: KPV may reduce inflammatory signs and could mask clinical progression; avoid initiating during severe systemic infections
  • Pregnancy / breastfeeding

Reported Side Effects

  • Fatigue
  • Injection‑site erythema
  • Transient GI upset
  • Hypersensitivity reactions have been reported anecdotally; their frequency and causal relationship to KPV remain uncertain.

Drivers of Diminished Response and Mitigation Strategies

KPV’s largely melanocortin-receptor-independent actions provide a plausible rationale for a low risk of classical receptor desensitization. [3,4,7] KPV’s small tripeptide structure provides a plausible rationale for limited immunogenicity. [27] Adaptation within inflammatory pathways and immune homeostasis could plausibly reduce response over time.

Community reports describe continuous KPV use without cycling.

Biological Mechanism

KPV is the C-terminal tripeptide (Lys-Pro-Val) of α-melanocyte-stimulating hormone. Its anti-inflammatory effects plausibly involve PepT1-mediated uptake into intestinal epithelial and immune cells, followed by inhibition of NF-κB and MAPK signaling and reduced inflammatory cytokine production. These effects are largely independent of melanocortin-receptor activation. Human bronchial epithelial cell studies also support a plausible mechanism involving inhibition of NF-κB nuclear entry. [3–5,7] KPV plausibly supports an M2 repair-associated macrophage phenotype; this effect was demonstrated in mice receiving the engineered oral proKPV formulation. [9] KPV likely reduces neutrophil recruitment. [3,5]

KPV plausibly supports intestinal epithelial and mucosal-barrier integrity through reduced inflammatory signaling. In mice with colitis, the engineered oral proKPV formulation restored occludin and ZO-1 expression and reduced intestinal permeability. These findings support a plausible barrier-repair rationale for ordinary KPV. [4,6,9] KPV likely reduces intestinal inflammation, with plausible support for mucosal healing and broader anti-inflammatory applications. [3–7,14,15]
